\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{ch:abstract}

This thesis explores the limits of an interpretable, near-annotation-free pipeline
for detecting and completing cars in LiDAR point clouds. The approach uses classical
geometry where it is reliable (ground removal and HDBSCAN density clustering) and
lightweight learned models only where discrimination is necessary (a binary
classifier and a PCN completion network).

On the detection side, the pipeline achieves high precision (\num{0.905}) but is
structurally recall-limited (\num{0.730} at a point-IoU of \num{0.3}, on cars with
enough returns to be scorable), a result validated across all eleven labelled
sequences to rule out model-selection bias. The thesis root-causes this recall
shortfall directly to density clustering fragmenting single vehicles, a limitation
that multiple repair strategies failed to overcome. Synthetic classifier pretraining
is further shown to be redundant once real training clusters are plentiful.

On the completion side, the thesis demonstrates that standard reference-based Chamfer
distance is invalid on real accumulated LiDAR because the raw partial input
artificially scores best. In its place, this work defines and validates a
leakage-free, donor-frame occluded-side coverage metric. Evaluated on this metric,
the completion network recovers genuinely unseen surface well above a symmetry-mirror
baseline and improves amodal bounding boxes on static, gate-passed cars---a benefit
that partially replicates under a pre-registered held-out test. Ultimately, the thesis
contributions are methodological and diagnostic: a validated real-data completion
metric, a root-cause account of why the classical clustering detector is
recall-limited, and evidence that synthetic pretraining is redundant at scale.

\chapter{Discussion and Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

The preceding chapters reported results; this one interprets them and closes the
thesis. It states, in one place, what the evidence in this thesis does and does not
support, and then names every
limitation the work carries --- including the ones an examiner is most likely to raise.
A limitation that the thesis names, quantifies,
and bounds is a defended limitation; one that a committee surfaces first is not. Every
weakness discussed here is already measured elsewhere in the document, and is
cross-referenced to the finding that quantifies it. Nothing in this chapter softens a
result reported earlier; it draws the boundary around each result precisely.

We first summarise what the thesis establishes and how far each claim reaches
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-what-holds}). We then work through the limitations in four
groups: threats to the validity of the evaluation itself
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-eval-validity}), the scope of the completion claims
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-completion-scope}), the ground-truth and dataset assumptions
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-gt}), and the architectural and operational limits of the
approach (Section~\ref{sec:disc-architectural}). Section~\ref{sec:disc-generalises}
separates what generalises from what is specific to the sequences studied. The
chapter then concludes the thesis: it answers the two research questions in the
form they were asked (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-rqs}), restates the contributions
(Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-contributions}), and turns the most consequential of the
boundaries drawn here into future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future}).

\section{Established results}
\label{sec:disc-what-holds}

Two threads carry the strongest evidence, both belonging to the metric contribution. The
first is methodological: the donor-frame occluded-side coverage metric (Section~\ref{sec:donor-validation}). We are not aware of a prior metric
that scores a real LiDAR car's completion against donor frames of the same static
instance, and it comes with the demonstration
that the obvious alternative --- Chamfer distance
against accumulated pseudo-ground-truth --- is invalid, because accumulated LiDAR is
itself one-sided and so rewards under-completion (the raw partial scored the lowest
Chamfer distance on every real example). This is a measured negative
result about metrics, not an opinion. The second is empirical and rests on that metric:
completion adds genuinely unseen surface (median coverage of never-observed surface
within \SI{0.1}{\metre} of \num{0.304} on sequence~08, seven times the symmetry-mirror
baseline of \num{0.043} and against a raw-partial baseline of \num{0.000}) and improves the amodal bounding box --- the final completed box reaches a median
bird's-eye-view IoU of \num{0.771} against the amodal ground-truth box, up from
\num{0.725} for the raw partial, with the gains concentrated on width, height, and
centre where the sensor saw least --- and both headline claims survive a
pre-registered held-out replication, with named coverage caveats
(outcome \textsc{partially holds}; Section~\ref{sec:disc-eval-validity}).

The detection pipeline is a strong engineering result rather than a claim of
state-of-the-art accuracy. It reaches precision \num{0.905}, recall \num{0.730}, and
$F_1$~\num{0.808} on the full sequence~08 (mean IoU of matched detections \num{0.912};
recall denominator: ground-truth car instances with at least \num{10} points surviving
preprocessing, Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}), and it does so with an interpretable, near-annotation-free
clustering front end on modest hardware. Its recall shortfall is not mysterious: it is
root-caused to HDBSCAN splitting large, close cars into fragments (Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}), and that
diagnosis is itself a finding. Two secondary findings round out the account: the
recall bottleneck is clustering fragmentation with a recoverable-ceiling quantification, and the sim-to-real classifier gap is total and symmetric, which
makes synthetic pretraining redundant once \num{420000} real clusters are available --- a negative result that replaces, rather than supports, the
two-stage-training contribution proposed at the outset.

The rest of this chapter is about how far each of these reaches.

\section{Threats to the validity of the evaluation}
\label{sec:disc-eval-validity}

\paragraph{Detection is cross-validated, and the selection exposure is bounded to precision.}
SemanticKITTI provides labels only for sequences \numrange{00}{10}; the official test
sequences \numrange{11}{21} are unlabelled, so no labelled sequence can be reserved as a
conventional held-out detection test set. The final classifier was historically
selected on sequence~08, which is also the sequence at which the pipeline is reported in
detail --- a model-selection exposure we address directly rather than only disclose. We
ran leave-one-sequence-out (LOSO) cross-validation over all eleven labelled sequences
(Section~\ref{sec:eval-loso}): each fold trains a classifier on ten sequences and scores
the full held-out eleventh, so every fold's classifier is blind to the sequence it
scores, with checkpoint selection removed entirely (fixed epoch budget, last-epoch
checkpoint). This bounds the exposure empirically, and the bound is small and one-sided.
The fold-08 classifier, blind to sequence~08, reaches recall \num{0.737} against the sequence-08
operating point's \num{0.730} --- marginally higher, not lower --- so \emph{recall was not
inflated} by having selected the classifier on the reporting sequence. The optimism that
does exist is confined to precision and is about two points: fold-08 precision is
\num{0.881} versus the final \num{0.905}. This is exactly what the stage decomposition
predicts (Section~\ref{sec:results-ablations}): geometric clustering alone reaches recall
\num{0.775}, the classifier lowers it to \num{0.677}, and the track filter recovers it to
\num{0.730}, so the classifier's realised recall contribution is a
small band and the dominant recall limit is the upstream clustering ceiling (\num{0.775}
before the classifier acts) --- imposed by clustering, independent of the classifier, and
holding on sequence~00 as well (Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}). The
model-selection structure therefore touched the precision and the classifier's few-point
recall contribution, not the clustering-imposed ceiling that accounts for most of the
missed cars; cross-validation now shows the touch was worth about two precision points and
no recall. The pooled cross-validated operating point over all eleven sequences
(precision \num{0.874}, recall \num{0.719}, $F_1$~\num{0.789}) sits within roughly one
point of the sequence-08 operating point on every metric. One residual remains and is
bounded: the classical \texttt{PIPELINE\_CONFIG} was historically tuned on sequence~00, so
the seq-00 fold retains classical-stage familiarity (though not classifier leakage);
dropping it lowers pooled recall only from \num{0.719} to \num{0.697} (a \num{0.022}
delta), so the seq-00 tuning does not drive the aggregate. What was previously a breadth
caveat --- a headline on a single sequence, with per-sequence recall variation
uncharacterised --- is likewise resolved: LOSO reports every sequence, and the spread is
real, from recall \num{0.336} on the sparse highway sequence~01 to \num{0.761} on dense
urban sequence~00 (macro mean \num{0.633}~$\pm$~\num{0.121}; precision far steadier at
\num{0.840}~$\pm$~\num{0.070}). The completion side is held out by a different and
stronger construction: its headline claims are re-tested on sequence~00 under a
pre-registration committed before any sequence-00 evaluation was run, and
the completion model was trained on synthetic data and never on any evaluation sequence.
The detection findings and the completion contribution thus still carry different evidential weights,
but detection is now a cross-validated result with a quantified, precision-only selection
exposure, not a bare validation-set number.

\paragraph{Coverage gaps in the held-out completion replication.}
The pre-registered replication returned the outcome \textsc{partially holds}, not
\textsc{holds}, and the reasons are specific and named. Both primary metrics were
decisive wins on the held-out sequence~00 (BEV IoU \num{0.739}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.766},
$p=\num{1.6e-3}$; donor coverage at \SI{0.1}{\metre} \num{0.000}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.413},
$p\approx0$), so the value of completion does replicate. Two gaps prevent a
full \textsc{holds}. First, the \emph{long band is empty}: no car of at least
\SI{4.6}{\metre} survived the well-observed-static-car construction on sequence~00
(bands: compact \num{17}, normal \num{28}, long \num{0}), so the per-car length estimate's
behaviour on long cars is untestable on the held-out sequence. This is a coverage gap in
the replication, not a weak result --- the sequence~08 long-band evidence stands --- and
the downgrade to \textsc{partially holds} follows the pre-registered taxonomy's
``caveat named'' clause rather than a metric failure. Second, the compact-band
hallucination guard fails on the held-out sequence --- a real failure, not only a naming
convention --- which the next section treats both as a metric-design point and as a
completion limitation in its own right. And the replication rests on a single held-out
sequence: sequence~00 is the one labelled sequence held out from the completion model
(which was trained on synthetic data, never on a labelled sequence), so the completion
value is replicated once, not across a range of sequences.

\section{Scope of the completion claims}
\label{sec:disc-completion-scope}

\paragraph{The accuracy claims are for static cars.}
The two quantitative completion metrics --- donor coverage and amodal-box overlap ---
both require a static car by construction. Donor coverage scores completion of one
frame's cluster against other frames of the \emph{same} static car, which only exists
if the car does not move; the amodal box is built from accumulated static-car points.
Moving cars therefore cannot be scored by either metric. We report them separately and
only as a plausibility check: on the final sequence-08 output, moving car tracks
complete into plausible car boxes at essentially the same rate as static ones
(\SI{57.9}{\percent}, \num{11}/\num{19}, versus \SI{53.7}{\percent}, \num{225}/\num{419}). This supports the statement that motion does not \emph{degrade}
completion plausibility --- expected, since completion is single-frame and carries no
motion smear --- but it is a plausibility parity result, not an accuracy result. We never
claim mover completion \emph{accuracy}; the metrics that would license that claim do not
apply to movers. One direction of the plausibility measure's bias is worth naming, because
it works in the mover cohort's favour. The plausibility flag reads axis-aligned bounding-box
dimensions, which overstate the width and length of any car whose heading is not aligned
with the sensor axes, pushing such a car toward the ``too wide'' edge of the plausible band. Moving cars are the sub-population most likely to be non-axis-aligned ---
static cars in these sequences are overwhelmingly parked parallel to the roadside, whereas
movers include vehicles turning at junctions and changing lanes --- so this bias should fall
disproportionately on movers, marking some plausible mover completions as implausible. To
the extent that holds, the \SI{57.9}{\percent} mover rate is an even more conservative lower
bound than the static \SI{53.7}{\percent}, and the parity conclusion (motion does not
degrade plausibility) is if anything understated. We flag this as the expected direction of
the bias rather than a measured orientation distribution, which we did not compute.

\paragraph{Input quality, not model choice, bounds the metric's scope.}
The donor metric scores completion only on clean, gate-passed single-object clusters, and
that scope is not incidental: whether a detected car reaches completion as such a clean
input is decided upstream, by the same clustering step that caps recall
(Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}). An eleven-sequence ablation isolates the effect of
input quality. Rendering completion with the L-shape completion input gate
switched off and then on, and scoring each output by the \emph{plausibility box} --- the
footprint length/width/height check, a different instrument from the donor metric --- the
fraction of completions whose footprint lands in the plausible-car range rises from
\num{0.473} to \num{0.686} pooled, a \num{+0.213} shift that is positive on every one of
the eleven sequences (per-sequence \num{+0.081} to \num{+0.319}). The gate carries a real
cost, which we report rather than hide: it drops \SI{15.5}{\percent} of otherwise-plausible
cars (\num{356} pooled) as false rejections. The complementary result is that the
completion \emph{network} does not carry this benefit: on the same clean-gated tracks, two
completion architectures (PCN and PoinTr) produce statistically indistinguishable real-data
plausibility (\num{18}/\num{26} versus \num{16}/\num{26}, the difference threshold noise), because the residual centroid-estimation gap is architecture-independent. Input quality, set by segmentation, therefore governs whether completion is
usable at all, whereas the choice of completion model --- across the two tried --- does
not. This is the segmentation bottleneck; we raise it as context for the metric's scope ---
the donor metric earns clean numbers precisely on the inputs the L-shape gate admits, and
widening that admission is a segmentation problem, not a completion one --- rather than as a
result the metric itself produces.

\paragraph{The per-car length estimate falls back too often.}
The final length geometry uses a per-car length estimate (the \num{90}th percentile of
gate-passed longitudinal extents over a track, plus \SI{0.12}{\metre}), which fixed the
compact-overshoot problem of the earlier fixed prior (compact signed length error
\num{+0.295}~$\rightarrow$~\num{+0.063}~m). But it falls back to the fixed
population prior of \SI{4.14}{\metre} on tracks with fewer than five gate-passed frames,
and that fallback fires on \SI{23.0}{\percent} of final completed tracks
(\num{119}/\num{518} on the regenerated sequence-08 output). This is well
above a comfortable threshold and is not solved: on those tracks the length geometry is
the population prior, not a per-car estimate, and it reintroduces the compact-overshoot
risk the per-car estimate was built to remove. We measured the fallback frequency rather
than assuming it small, and we carry it as a live limitation. What the rate is \emph{not}
is an unquantified risk sitting outside the reported result. The headline box-quality gain
(median BEV IoU \num{0.725}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.771}) is measured on the
final sequence-08 output --- the same \num{518} tracks of which the \num{119} fallback
tracks are a subset --- so the fallback cost is already \emph{inside} that number, not
additional to it. The \num{0.771} is therefore what the pipeline delivers \emph{with} the
fallback firing on one track in four; it is a measured ceiling on the aggregate, not a
threat to it. Two things bound the residual further. The fallback fires precisely on
short, few-frame tracks, which are the \emph{sparsest} and typically most distant cars,
where the amodal box is least certain and the completed box contributes least to any
downstream use; and the per-band hallucination guard already isolates the worst case ---
on the fallback tracks the compact out-of-box ratio returns to the full
\num{100}-times-baseline violation, a bound we report rather than average
away. The honest reading is that the fallback caps how much better the aggregate could be,
and that a mitigation --- a lower gate, or a per-band prior for the fallback path ---
is concrete future work, not a defect hidden inside the headline.

\paragraph{The residual under-extension is a characterised, length-dependent limit.}
Even with an unbiased length estimate, completed clouds remain slightly too short at the
far end, and the amount needed to correct this scales with car length. We tried to remove
it: a pre-registered intervention (decoupling the output radius from the length push, plus
a synthetic-calibrated length-axis fill factor) fixed the target on normal and long cars
but over-extended compacts, failing the pre-registered compact non-regression guard on
both sequences. We report this as a finding: the compensation is
irreducibly length-dependent, so a single global fix cannot remove it without regressing
another band, and pursuing per-length compensation would need more cars than the data
safely supports. The far end is therefore the weakest-covered region of the completed car,
and we say so.

\paragraph{The completion model never saw real LiDAR.}
The completion network was trained only on synthetic KITTI-like partial renders. That is
what keeps its real-data evaluation independent of its training data (Section~\ref{sec:disc-eval-validity}), but it is
also a limitation, and we name it as the completion counterpart of the classifier
sim-to-real gap of Section~\ref{sec:disc-what-holds}. The model never saw real sensor
statistics, and the residual flatness of real completions on the
sparsest, most one-sided inputs traces to exactly this remaining domain gap (alongside imperfect
centre estimation). Closing it would call for real-data fine-tuning, which is itself a
documented negative-result line in this project and is left to
future work; the synthetic-only training is thus at once the source of the metric's
validity and a ceiling on real-data crispness.

\paragraph{Compacts are where completion strays, and the guard was band-blind to it.}
This limitation has two faces --- a completion defect and a metric-design lesson --- and
they must be kept distinct. The completion defect: the final completion sprays points
outside the true box on \emph{compact} cars. The metric lesson: the original out-of-box
hallucination guard was a pooled median across all cars, and that pooled statistic was
blind to exactly this band-localised failure. The fixed \SI{4.14}{\metre} length prior
--- sized using that same pooled guard --- hallucinates on compact cars
at roughly \num{100} times the compact band's mirrored baseline (\num{0.0433} versus
\num{0.0004}), a failure the pooled number could not see because the other \num{32} of
\num{40} cars sit near it. The final per-car length estimate, which
replaced that fixed prior, reduces the compact hallucination to about \num{16} times the
baseline (\num{0.0065}) --- roughly a sevenfold reduction, not elimination --- but on the
\SI{23}{\percent} of tracks that fall back to the fixed \SI{4.14}{\metre} prior (the
fallback of the previous paragraph) the full \num{100}-times compact
hallucination returns. So the defect is real, is worst on the fallback tracks, and is not
eliminated on any track.

We report ratios rather than a pass/fail bit because the guard bar is strict and no
final configuration clears it on compacts. The bar is that the out-of-box fraction must
not exceed the band's mirrored baseline, so any ratio above \num{1} is a failure and
higher is worse. By that bar sequence~08 fails worst (about \num{16} times with the
final estimate, \num{100} times on the fallback tracks), and the held-out sequence~00
confirms the defect does not clear off the tuning sequence either --- a milder but still
failing \num{1.8} times (completed \num{0.009} versus mirrored \num{0.005}). The compact
mirrored baseline is itself near \num{0.0004} --- mirroring a compact car barely leaves its
box --- so that band's bar is effectively ``do not leave the box at all,'' and no method
reaches it. We therefore treat the guard as failing on compacts and report the ratios as
they stand, because tuning the metric until it passes would defeat the
purpose of a hallucination guard. The per-band guard is what makes the defect visible
where a pooled median hid it --- but the underlying fact is a completion
accuracy limitation on compact cars, not merely a property of the metric.

\section{Ground-truth and dataset assumptions}
\label{sec:disc-gt}

\paragraph{The amodal ground truth is constructed, and cannot be externally validated.}
The completion evaluation compares against an amodal (full-extent) box built by
accumulating a static car's points across frames, because no external amodal reference
exists for these sequences. We attempted the obvious cross-check --- KITTI raw tracklets
for the drive corresponding to odometry sequence~08 --- and established that it is
impossible: that drive (\texttt{2011\_09\_30\_drive\_0028}) has no tracklet annotations on
the official archive (verified against a control drive that does), and neither do the
neighbouring candidate drives. The amodal ground truth therefore rests on three defences
rather than an external reference: the paired evaluation design tolerates reference noise
because it compares raw versus completed against the \emph{same} box; the construction
guards enforce viewpoint coverage and near-zero motion; and the dimension distribution is
sane (median L~\num{4.14}~/~W~\num{1.75}~/~H~\num{1.47}~\si{\metre}, consistent with
published car statistics). The amodal ground truth is not error-free; the claim is that
the paired design is robust to the errors it can plausibly contain.

\paragraph{Recall against all annotated cars, and the two ``$\geq$10-point'' rules.}
The headline recall denominator counts ground-truth car instances with at least \num{10}
points surviving preprocessing (z-filter, voxelisation, denoising, ground removal). This
excludes cars that are too sparse or too distant to survive the front end, so the headline
recall is a recall against \emph{eligible} cars, not against all annotated cars. We
quantify the gap rather than leave it implicit: on a stride-20 sample of sequence~08,
\SI{25.5}{\percent} of all annotated cars (and \SI{14.6}{\percent} of cars with at least
\num{10} \emph{raw} points) are excluded by the eligibility rule, so recall against all
annotated cars is approximately \num{0.54}. The two ``$\geq$10-point''
thresholds are different quantities --- one counts raw label points, the other counts
points surviving preprocessing --- and they are not interchangeable; we keep them
distinct wherever both appear. Stating the \num{0.54} figure alongside the \num{0.730}
eligible-car recall is the honest way to report the operating range: the pipeline detects
about three eligible cars in four, and about half of all annotated cars including those it
never receives enough points to see.

\paragraph{Label quality is assumed.}
All ground-truth counts, matches, and the amodal construction assume SemanticKITTI's
semantic and instance labels are correct. We did not audit label quality, and mislabelled
or mis-instanced cars would propagate into both the detection metrics and the amodal
ground truth. This is a standard assumption for work on this dataset, but it is an
assumption, and we name it.

\section{Architectural and operational limits}
\label{sec:disc-architectural}

\paragraph{Density clustering has no notion of objectness.}
The recall ceiling is architectural, not a tuning failure (Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}). Coarsening the
clustering voxel to \SI{0.10}{\metre} recovered part of the loss
(recall \num{0.699}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.730}) by preventing intra-car
splits, but coarsening further would begin fusing adjacent cars, and the
clustering-method benchmark shows that no fixed-radius density clusterer reaches even
HDBSCAN's recall at any tested radius. The residual limit is that
density-based clustering groups points by proximity and density alone, with no model of
what a car is, so it cannot be tuned to the recall of a learned object detector. Lifting
this ceiling would require replacing the clustering stage with a learned detector, which
is a different system and is left to future work (Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future}). The value of Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis} is to
have located the ceiling precisely --- clustering, splitting rather than
classification-or-merging, and lower than first claimed --- rather than to have removed it.

Retaining the classical front-end despite this ceiling is a deliberate choice, not an
oversight, and it is the choice the thesis exists to evaluate. The question posed at the
outset (Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction}) is how far an \emph{interpretable,
near-annotation-free} pipeline can go: the clustering front end proposes objects with no
bounding-box supervision and with every stage inspectable, and the only learned component
is a binary classifier trained on auto-mined weak labels. A learned detector would lift the
recall ceiling, but it would do so by importing exactly what this pipeline is built to
avoid --- box-level annotation for training and an opaque, non-inspectable proposal
stage --- so it answers a different research question rather than improving the answer to
this one. The recall ceiling is therefore best read as the \emph{measured price} of the
annotation-free, interpretable design: the contribution is not that the price is low, but
that it is now quantified (a clustering-imposed ceiling of \num{0.775} before any learned
stage acts, splitting \SIrange{31}{37}{\percent} of cars), root-caused, and shown to be
architectural rather than a tuning artefact. A committee weighing the recall penalty
against a learned detector is weighing two different systems with different labelling
budgets and different interpretability, and the thesis supplies the number that makes that
trade explicit rather than assuming either side of it.

\paragraph{The operational domain is dense scenes; sparse highway is the weak edge.}
The leave-one-sequence-out table (Section~\ref{sec:eval-loso}) makes an operational-domain
limit explicit that a single dense-urban reporting sequence could not. Recall on
sequence~01 --- the highway drive --- falls to \num{0.336}, against \num{0.761} on the
dense-urban sequence~00 and a macro mean of \num{0.633}. This is a genuine restriction and
we state it as one: the pipeline is at its best in dense street scenes and is markedly
weaker on high-speed, sparse highway data. The cause is mostly intrinsic and partly tuning.
It is intrinsic because highway cars are predominantly distant, and the recall-by-distance
analysis (Section~\ref{sec:results-distance}) shows recall collapsing with range as returns
per car thin out (to \num{0.196} beyond \SI{40}{\metre}); a car reduced to a handful of
points cannot be clustered into a matchable object by any density method. It is partly
tuning because the clustering front end --- the \SI{0.10}{\metre} cluster voxel and the
HDBSCAN density parameters --- was set on the dense sequence~08, so it is adapted to urban
point densities rather than highway ones. The failure mode is a conservative \emph{miss},
not a false alarm: sequence~01 precision stays high at \num{0.951} (only \num{28} false
positives), so the sparse regime costs recovered cars, not spurious detections. Deploying
the detector on sustained highway or otherwise sparse data would therefore need either a
range-adaptive clustering front end or the learned detector of the preceding paragraph; as
it stands, its operational design domain is effectively dense-scene, and the LOSO spread is
the honest measurement of that boundary rather than an average that hides it.

\paragraph{The system is offline by design.}
The pipeline runs at about \SI{623.5}{\milli\second} per frame on the reference hardware
(\num{1.6}~frames/s; promoted-configuration full-sequence timing, Section~\ref{sec:results-runtime}), dominated
by the classical CPU stages (HDBSCAN alone is roughly \SI{59}{\percent}). This is offline,
not real-time, and real-time operation is explicitly out of scope. The runtime work that
was done reached this operating point and established that the
$\leq\SI{400}{\milli\second}$ target is not reachable through the authorised
exact-output and voxel-resolution tiers, because the post-ground object cloud is
intrinsically sparse; GPU clustering was deliberately not pursued. A real-time variant is
future work, not a claim of this thesis.

\paragraph{Tracking is used but not quantified.}
The centroid tracker is evaluated only indirectly. It feeds the per-car length estimate
and the completion aggregation, and its five-gate-passed-frame requirement is what drives
the \SI{23}{\percent} length-estimate fallback: identity switches or
fragmented tracks raise that fallback rate directly, and thereby the compact-overshoot
risk it carries. Yet we report no multi-object-tracking accuracy metric (no MOTA, IDF1, or
equivalent) --- the tracker is used as a filter and an aggregator, not assessed as a
tracker --- so its contribution to the fallback rate, and any completion error it
propagates, is not separately measured. Quantifying tracking robustness is future work.

\section{Generality and sequence-specific factors}
\label{sec:disc-generalises}

The results split into those that should transfer beyond the sequences studied and
those tied to them. The detection operating point is the broadest: it is
cross-validated across all eleven labelled sequences (Section~\ref{sec:eval-loso}), so its
pooled figure and its per-sequence spread are measured, not extrapolated. The
\emph{mechanisms} behind the completion and recall-diagnosis results generalise more
narrowly, and not all with the same evidential force. Two transfer by argument rather
than by sample: the demonstration that accumulated-pseudo-ground-truth Chamfer is invalid
follows deductively from the one-sidedness of accumulated LiDAR, which is a property of the
sensor and not of any sequence; and the donor metric's construction is dataset-agnostic by
definition wherever static cars are observed from multiple frames. The third --- the recall
diagnosis that density-based clustering splits large, close, dense cars --- is weaker: it
is an empirical, inductive result, measured on the two sequences studied. It is consistent
across both, and its cause (HDBSCAN cutting along internal density variation within a dense
return) is mechanistic, so we expect it to be a property of the clustering algorithm and
the sensor geometry rather than of sequence~08 --- but that is an inference from two
sequences, not a proven invariant, and we state it as one. The \emph{constants} are more
local. The length prior (\SI{4.14}{\metre}), the length-estimate
quantile and offset, and the geometric-filter thresholds were tuned on sequence~08, and
while the held-out replication shows the completion \emph{value} transfers, the specific
constants are sequence-derived and should be re-checked before applying the system to a new
sensor or a new class. The completion accuracy claims are scoped to static, gate-passed
cars in the compact and normal length bands; the long band is supported on sequence~08 but
was untestable on the held-out sequence, and moving cars are covered only by the
plausibility check. Within that scope the claims are pre-registered and replicated; outside
it, they are hypotheses for future work rather than results.

This is the boundary the thesis draws. The results in
Section~\ref{sec:disc-what-holds} hold within it, each limitation above marks where it
ends, and the future work of Section~\ref{sec:conclusion-future} turns the most
consequential of those boundaries --- the clustering
objectness ceiling, the length-estimate fallback rate, and the missing long-band and
mover accuracy evidence --- into concrete next steps.

Having drawn the boundary around each result, the chapter now closes the thesis.
This work studied an interpretable, near-annotation-free pipeline for detecting and
completing cars in LiDAR point clouds, and asked what such a system can and cannot
establish. The detection side is a training-free clustering-plus-classifier pipeline whose
recall was root-caused rather than tuned away; the completion side is a learned model
whose value on real data had to be measured before it could be claimed, because no valid
real-data metric existed at the outset. What follows states the answers in the form the
questions were asked, restates what the thesis contributes, and turns the most
consequential of those boundaries into future work.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:conclusion-rqs}

Chapter~\ref{ch:introduction} posed two research questions, both about measuring completion on real LiDAR. Each is
answered by a specific chapter, at the reach the evidence actually supports; a pair of diagnostic findings about the
detection front-end, which the investigation produced along the way, follows the two answers.

\paragraph{RQ1 --- evaluating completion on real LiDAR.}
The measurement question is answered in Section~\ref{sec:donor-validation}. Reference-based Chamfer distance against
an accumulated pseudo-ground-truth is \emph{invalid} on real cars: because the accumulated
reference is itself occluded on the same side as the input, the raw partial cloud scores
best on that metric, so a lower Chamfer distance rewards under-completion rather than
reconstruction. The thesis instead defines a donor-frame occluded-side
coverage metric: it completes a car from one frame's pipeline cluster and scores how much
of the surface seen only in \emph{other} frames of the same static car the completion
recovers, paired with a hallucination guard against surface invented outside the car's
amodal box. The metric passes a four-item validation gate, and the per-band form of that
guard exposed a failure the original pooled statistic was blind to --- a metric-design
lesson about band-blind gates, not an erratum to the direction it validated. This is a validated way to score completion quality on
real LiDAR in this setting.

\paragraph{RQ2 --- does completion recover unseen surface, and does the benefit hold out?}
Also answered in Section~\ref{sec:donor-validation}, and reported exactly as it fell. On real sequence-08 cars the
donor metric shows that PCN recovers genuinely occluded surface several times above the
symmetry-mirror baseline (occluded-side coverage at \SI{0.1}{\metre} rising from
\num{0.000} for the raw partial and \num{0.043} for the mirror baseline to \num{0.304} for
the completion, per-car medians), rather than merely redistributing the points it was
given, and the completed cloud improves downstream amodal bounding boxes on static,
gate-passed cars in the compact and normal length bands. Under a
pre-registered test on the held-out sequence~00, the benefit \textsc{partially holds}: the two primary metrics replicate on the held-out data
(BEV IoU \num{0.739}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.766}, $p=\num{1.6e-3}$; donor coverage
\num{0.000}~$\rightarrow$~\num{0.413}), but a coverage gap prevents a full \textsc{holds} ---
the long length band was empty on sequence~00 (no car of at least \SI{4.6}{\metre} survived
the static-car construction), so long-band behaviour was untestable off the tuning sequence.
The compact-band hallucination guard also fails here, as it does on sequence~08. Moving cars
are covered by a plausibility check only, never an accuracy claim; that
plausibility scoping is stated in Section~\ref{sec:results-movers} and the held-out verdict in Section~\ref{sec:results-heldout}, and
neither is softened here.

\paragraph{Along the way --- what limits recall, and whether synthetic pretraining is needed.}
These are diagnostic findings the investigation produced rather than questions posed at the outset; the first is developed
in Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}, the second in Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}. Recall is limited not by
the classifier but by the clustering stage: HDBSCAN splits single cars into multiple
fragments, with \SIrange{31}{37}{\percent} of ground-truth cars split, and
the classifier's role is to buy precision, which it does at a cost to recall that the track
filter then partly recovers (geometric-only recall \num{0.775}, classifier \num{0.677},
track filter \num{0.730}). A search of repair strategies --- larger
minimum cluster size, fragment merging, adaptive clustering, BEV clustering, temporal accumulation,
threshold lowering --- fails to generalise, each for a structural reason, and is reported
as a chain of negative findings. The one lever that helped was the
clustering resolution itself, which lifted recall from \num{0.699} to \num{0.730} and
showed the earlier ``hard limit'' was partly a resolution artifact; a
residual limit remains because density clustering has no notion of objectness. Recall is
stated throughout against the car-only $\geq\!10$-surviving-point denominator of
Section~\ref{sec:eval-protocol}; against all annotated cars the effective figure is lower (approximately
\num{0.54}). On the second half, synthetic pretraining is \emph{redundant}: a
from-scratch classifier matches a synthetically pretrained one once real training clusters
are plentiful (of order \num{420000} mined clusters), and a cross-domain matrix shows the
sim-to-real gap is total and symmetric --- car $F_1$ collapses to \num{0.000} in every
off-diagonal cell.

\section{Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusion-contributions}

Restated compactly, and pointing back to the answers above rather than re-arguing them, the
thesis makes a single scientific contribution, methodological rather than architectural:
\textbf{a validated metric for completion quality on real LiDAR} --- the
demonstration that accumulated-pseudo-ground-truth Chamfer is invalid on real cars, and the
donor-frame occluded-side coverage metric that replaces it, with its hallucination guard and
validation gate --- through which completion is shown to recover genuinely occluded surface
and improve amodal boxes on static, gate-passed cars, a benefit that partially holds on a
pre-registered held-out sequence (Section~\ref{sec:donor-validation}).

Two diagnostic findings sit alongside it:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A root-cause account of the recall bottleneck:} recall is clustering-bound,
  not classifier-bound, and the accompanying chain of negative repair results is reported as
  findings in their own right (Section~\ref{sec:recall-analysis}).
  \item \textbf{Evidence that synthetic pretraining is redundant for the classifier at
  scale} (Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}).
\end{enumerate}

Alongside these, the thesis carries engineering contributions --- the modular pipeline
and its runtime characterisation, the amodal
ground-truth box builder, and the reproducibility infrastructure --- and a set of documented
negative-result lines (architecture swaps and real-data fine-tuning for completion; a sparse-fallback length model).

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:conclusion-future}

The limitations above named the boundaries of the present results. Three of them are the natural next
steps, each already scoped by a finding rather than left open.

\paragraph{A real-time variant.} The pipeline runs offline at roughly
\SI{623.5}{\milli\second} per scan (\num{1.6}~frames/s; Section~\ref{sec:results-runtime}), dominated by the
classical CPU stages. The runtime work that was done established that
the $\leq\SI{400}{\milli\second}$ target is not reachable through the authorised
exact-output and voxel-resolution tiers, because the post-ground object cloud is
intrinsically sparse. An implementation floor must be distinguished from an algorithmic
one here: the residual $\sim$\SI{185}{\milli\second} attributed to clustering
(Section~\ref{sec:results-runtime}) is the cost of the \emph{Python} HDBSCAN, not of the
HDBSCAN \emph{algorithm}: a native (C++) or GPU-accelerated HDBSCAN implementation would
attack that floor mechanically, without changing what is computed, and is the most direct
speed path. The tempting shortcut --- swapping HDBSCAN for a faster GPU density clusterer
such as \texttt{cuML} DBSCAN --- is \emph{not} a free substitution, and this thesis has
the measurement to say so: the clustering benchmark showed that no
fixed-radius density clusterer, DBSCAN included, reaches HDBSCAN's recall at any tested
radius, because it is HDBSCAN's density-adaptive linkage that recovers cars a single global
radius cannot. A real-time variant must therefore preserve the HDBSCAN algorithm (via a
faster implementation) or replace the whole front end with a learned detector --- a
different system --- rather than trade the measured recall away for speed. Either path is
future work, not a claim of this thesis.

\paragraph{Real-data fine-tuning for completion.} The completion model was trained on
synthetic KITTI-like partials only, which is at once the source of the donor metric's
validity and a ceiling on real-data crispness: on the sparsest, most one-sided inputs the
completions stay flat, which traces to the remaining synthetic-to-real
domain gap. Closing it would call for real-data fine-tuning, which is itself a documented
negative-result line in this project; it is a contingency
direction, to be attempted only with the leakage controls that make real-data supervision
safe.

\paragraph{Long-band amodal ground truth on a second held-out sequence.} The held-out
replication (Section~\ref{sec:results-heldout}) partially held in part because the long length band was empty on
sequence~00 --- no car of at least \SI{4.6}{\metre} survived the well-observed-static-car
construction --- so the per-car length estimate's behaviour on long cars could not be tested
off the tuning sequence. Building amodal ground truth for a third sequence with long cars
present would close that single coverage gap and upgrade the long-band claim from a
sequence-08 result to a replicated one. It is deferred here as a bounded data-construction
task, not a change to the method.

\section{Closing remarks}
\label{sec:conclusion-closing}

On the completion side the thesis contributes a validated real-data metric and
pre-registered, partially replicated evidence that learned completion adds genuinely
unseen surface and improves amodal boxes on static cars. On the detection side it contributes a root-caused account of
why a training-free clustering detector is recall-limited, a chain of negative repair
results reported as findings, and the demonstration that synthetic pretraining is redundant
at scale. None of these rests on an additional point of metric improvement; each is defended
by explanation and, where the design allowed it, by a pre-registered test whose outcome was
reportable either way. That is the standard the work set for itself, and it is the standard
on which it asks to be judged.

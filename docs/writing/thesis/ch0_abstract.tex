\chapter*{Abstract}
\label{ch:abstract}

Automotive LiDAR sees every car from one side, so a perception system must both detect
partially observed vehicles and reason about the surface the sensor never reached.
End-to-end networks do this accurately but at the cost of dense point-wise supervision
and stage-by-stage opacity. This thesis takes the opposite starting point and builds a
modular detection-and-completion framework: classical geometry removes the ground
and clusters candidates, a small binary classifier keeps the cars, a centroid tracker
filters flicker, and a PCN network completes each detected vehicle. Every stage is
separately inspectable, and the framework requires zero additional manual annotation:
the classifier's training data is automatically mined from existing semantic datasets,
and the completion network is trained exclusively on synthetic shapes.

The framework is grounded on two public datasets: SemanticKITTI supplies real Velodyne
scans for detection, auto-mined supervision, and evaluation, while ShapeNetCore~v2
supplies synthetic complete shapes for training the completion network. Detection is evaluated end to end with leave-one-sequence-out
cross-validation across all eleven labelled sequences to rule out model-selection bias.
Completion, which has no clean ground truth on real LiDAR, is judged two ways: an
independent shape classifier trained on external complete-shape data tests
whether the completed real clouds read as cars, and established reference-free fidelity
metrics are reported honestly, including where they reward leaving the partial untouched.
The independent classifier accepts far more completed clouds than raw partials and about
as many as a dense static-accumulation reference, so completion produces car-plausible
shapes rather than merely denser blobs; fidelity, by contrast, penalises the added
surface, which is why the favorable real-data claim rests on plausibility. Along the way
we find detection recall limited by clustering fragmentation rather than the classifier,
and synthetic pretraining redundant once real training clusters are plentiful. The
thesis thus contributes an inspectable, reproducible framework for occluded-vehicle
detection and completion that requires zero additional manual annotation, and a
real-data completion assessment that avoids any self-made reference.
